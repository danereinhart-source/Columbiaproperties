Most Phoenix landlords spend their energy worrying about summer. That makes sense. AC failures, monsoon storms, and triple-digit heat cause most of the maintenance headaches in the Valley. Winter barely registers. Sunny days in the 60s and 70s do not exactly scream danger.

But the Phoenix area does get occasional overnight freezes, and homes here are not built with cold weather in mind. Pipes run along exterior walls, irrigation lines sit near the surface, backflow preventers stand exposed in the yard, and pool equipment sits outside with water in it. When temperatures dip below freezing, even for a few hours, those are the things that break.

The good news is that winter prep for a Phoenix rental is quick and inexpensive. Here is what to focus on.

Exposed Pipes and Hose Bibs

Any water line that runs outside or along an uninsulated exterior wall is at risk in a hard freeze. Common trouble spots include:

  • Outdoor hose bibs (spigots)
  • Water lines running to the water heater in a garage or exterior closet
  • Exposed supply lines near the main shutoff
  • Pipes feeding outdoor kitchens, misters, or fountains

Foam pipe insulation and hose bib covers are cheap and easy to install. Disconnect garden hoses from spigots before cold nights, since water trapped in the hose can freeze back into the fitting. If the property has misting systems, drain them for the season.

Backflow Preventers and Irrigation

This is the one that catches the most Phoenix owners off guard. Many homes have a brass backflow preventer assembly sticking up out of the ground near the house, connected to the irrigation system. These assemblies hold water and are completely exposed. A hard freeze can crack the body or damage internal parts, and repairs or replacement can be a real expense.

  • Insulate it. Insulated covers or bags made for backflow preventers are widely available at home improvement stores. You can also wrap it with pipe insulation and a towel, then cover it.
  • Check that the cover stays put. Winter winds can knock loose wraps off.
  • Adjust the irrigation timer for winter. Plants need far less water in the cooler months. Cutting back saves water and reduces the chance of running sprinklers during a freeze.
  • Avoid early morning watering on freeze nights. The coldest part of the night is often just before sunrise, which is when many timers are set to run.

If a backflow preventer does crack, you will often see water spraying or pooling once it thaws. Shutting off the irrigation supply until it can be repaired limits the damage.

Pool Equipment Freeze Protection

Pools are common in Phoenix rentals, and pool equipment does not handle freezing well. Water sitting still in pumps, filters, heaters, and above-ground plumbing can freeze and crack them.

  • Check for a freeze protection setting. Many automation systems and variable-speed pumps have a freeze protection feature that runs the pump when temperatures drop near freezing. Make sure it is turned on and working.
  • Run the pump on freeze nights. If there is no automated freeze protection, running the pump overnight keeps water moving, which helps prevent freezing.
  • Talk to your pool service. If a pool service maintains the property, ask whether they check freeze settings as part of their winter routine.

Frost-Sensitive Landscaping

Desert landscaping is tough, but some popular Valley plants do not tolerate frost well. Bougainvillea, lantana, many citrus trees, and some cacti and succulents can be damaged by a hard freeze.

  • Cover sensitive plants with frost cloth or old sheets before a freeze. Avoid plastic, which can trap cold and damage foliage.
  • Remove the covers in the morning once temperatures rise.
  • Do not prune frost-damaged plants right away. Damaged growth can help protect the rest of the plant from later cold snaps. Wait until the risk of frost has passed in spring.

Your lease should spell out who is responsible for landscaping care. If the tenant handles the yard, a friendly reminder helps. If you provide landscaping service, ask your crew to keep an eye on vulnerable plants.

Heating System Check

Phoenix homes typically use heat pumps or gas furnaces. Either way, the heating side of the system often sits unused for most of the year, so it is worth confirming it works before tenants need it.

  • Heat pumps: Make sure the system switches over to heat and warms the home. Many have backup electric heat strips for colder nights. If the unit seems to struggle, have a technician take a look.
  • Gas furnaces: Schedule an inspection. A technician should check the burners, ignition, and venting. Test carbon monoxide detectors in any home with gas appliances.
  • Air filters: A fresh filter going into winter helps the system run efficiently.

A heating failure in Phoenix is rarely an emergency the way an AC failure in July is, but tenants still expect a warm home on a cold night. A fall tune-up also catches small problems before the next summer. Our guide on HVAC maintenance in Arizona covers a year-round schedule.

Communicate With Tenants Before Cold Nights

Your tenants are your best defense against freeze damage, but many people who moved here from elsewhere assume Phoenix never freezes, and longtime residents may not think about it either. A short message when a freeze is forecast can prevent an expensive repair.

A good freeze reminder might ask tenants to:

  • Disconnect garden hoses from outdoor spigots
  • Leave the pool pump running overnight if it does not have automatic freeze protection
  • Cover frost-sensitive plants if they are responsible for the yard
  • Keep the heat on, even if they are traveling
  • Report any leaks or pooling water right away

Keep the tone helpful, not alarming. Most tenants appreciate the heads-up.

Vacant Properties Need Extra Attention

A vacant rental during a freeze is at higher risk because no one is there to notice a problem. If a property is between tenants in winter, make sure the heat stays on at a modest setting, outdoor fixtures are protected, pool freeze protection is working, and someone checks the property after any hard freeze. The same applies to seasonal homes, which is one reason home watch visits are valuable year-round.

Northern Arizona Is a Different Story

Everything above is about the low desert, where freezes are occasional and short. If you own a rental in Flagstaff or elsewhere in Northern Arizona, winter is a serious maintenance season. Real snow and hard freezes mean you need to think about fully winterizing vacant homes, snow removal, roof loads, ice dams, heating system reliability, and keeping pipes from freezing for days at a time.
