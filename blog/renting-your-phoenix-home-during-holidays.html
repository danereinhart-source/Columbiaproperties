Renting Your Phoenix Home During the Holidays: How to Beat Winter Vacancy

November 9, 2026 • 6 min read

Few rental owners want a vacancy in late November. Families are busy with holidays, travel takes people out of town, and most renters would rather not move between Thanksgiving and New Year's. Inquiries slow down, showings drop off, and an empty house can sit longer than you expected.

Slower does not mean stopped, though. People still need homes in the winter, and in the Phoenix area some of them are actively looking during exactly this stretch. With the right pricing, marketing, and lease strategy, you can fill a holiday vacancy and set yourself up so the next move-out lands at a better time of year.

Who Rents in the Winter?

The winter renter pool is smaller, but it tends to be motivated. People who are looking in December usually need to move, not just want to. Common winter renters include:

  • Job relocations and transfers. Companies hire and transfer employees year-round, and many new jobs start in January. Someone moving to the Valley for work often needs a home quickly and may not have time to wait for spring inventory.
  • Seasonal residents looking for longer stays. Some snowbirds want more than a few months. They may be testing out a part of the Valley before buying, or simply prefer an unfurnished lease. Depending on your goals, a seasonal resident can be a good fit for the right home.
  • People in life transitions. Home sales, new households, and changes in family situations do not follow the calendar.
  • Renters whose own leases end in winter. Every lease that started in a past winter creates another winter mover.
  • People relocating from colder places. For some, the middle of a northern winter is exactly when moving to Phoenix starts to sound like a great idea.

Price Realistically From Day One

In a slow season, your first two weeks on the market matter most. A listing that sits gets stale, and renters start wondering what is wrong with it. Pricing a little high and planning to drop later usually costs more than pricing right at the start.

Keep in mind that you are also competing with apartments. Phoenix-area apartment communities have been offering heavy move-in concessions, which Cushman & Wakefield reported were near a record high in Q2 2026. Winter renters comparing options will see those specials.

Think about the cost of waiting. For example, on a $2,200/month rental, a home that sits empty through December and January instead of leasing in early December loses roughly two months of rent, plus utilities and upkeep. That is far more than a modest price adjustment would have cost. Our guide on how to price your Phoenix rental walks through setting the right number, and a free rental analysis can give you a current read on your specific home.

If you would rather not lower the base rent, a targeted incentive can help. A reduced first month or a move-in date that lets the tenant avoid paying double rent during the holidays can make your listing stand out without resetting your rent for future renewals.

Use Lease Length to Fix the Problem for Next Time

Here is the strategy many owners miss. If you sign a standard twelve-month lease in December, the lease ends next December, and you may face the exact same slow-season vacancy again. Instead, adjust the lease length so the end date lands in a stronger leasing month.

In the Phoenix area, more renters tend to be moving in the spring and early summer, when school years end and people time their moves. So for a December move-in, consider:

  • A longer lease of around 15 to 18 months, ending the following spring or summer
  • A shorter initial lease of around 6 to 8 months, ending in the coming spring or summer, if the tenant prefers flexibility and you are comfortable with an earlier turnover

The longer option is usually the better fit, since it also reduces turnover costs. Either way, the goal is the same: when this tenant eventually moves out, you are marketing in a busy season, not another holiday lull. Our post on the cost of tenant turnover explains why every avoided vacancy matters.

Be upfront about the lease term in your listing and conversations so applicants are not surprised, and make sure the terms are clearly written in the lease.

Listing Photos That Work in Winter Light

Winter in the Valley is a great time to photograph a home. Skies are clear, the light is softer than in summer, and lawns and plants often look their best. A few tips:

  • Shoot in the middle of the day. Days are shorter and the sun sits lower, so late afternoon shots can turn dark quickly. Late morning to early afternoon usually gives the best light.
  • Turn on every light. Interior lights warm up rooms and fill in shadows.
  • Open blinds and curtains. Natural light makes rooms feel larger.
  • Show off the outdoor space. A patio, pool, or backyard in comfortable winter weather is a selling point for renters coming from colder places.
  • Skip the holiday decorations. If the home is occupied while you market it, ask permission before photographing, and avoid shots dominated by seasonal decor that will look dated in a few weeks.

Make sure the listing also mentions the features winter renters care about: heating type, fireplace if there is one, a heated pool or spa, and nearby amenities.

Be Flexible With Showings

Holiday schedules are messy. Renters may only be able to look on weekends, during a lunch break, or between family events. The easier you make it to see the home, the more showings you will get.

  • Offer evening and weekend appointments, including around holidays.
  • Make sure exterior and interior lights work, since it gets dark earlier.
  • Offer video tours for out-of-state relocations, who may need to commit before they arrive.
  • Respond to inquiries the same day. A motivated winter renter is likely contacting several listings at once.

Flexibility on showings should never mean cutting corners on screening. A rushed decision to fill a holiday vacancy can lead to much bigger problems later. Stick with a consistent process; our post on tenant screening best practices outlines what that looks like.

Keep the Home Rent-Ready

A vacant house in winter still needs care. Keep the heat on at a modest setting, maintain the pool and yard, and make sure the home is clean and fresh for every showing. It is also worth checking for anything a winter renter will notice right away, like drafty doors or a heating system that has not been serviced.
